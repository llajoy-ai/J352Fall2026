The Amazon Rainforest: Degradation Through the Years

Introduction To The Problem:

The Amazon Rainforest not only provides resources, habitats, ecosystem services and cultural value to the people and species living there, the trees also sequester carbon from the atmosphere. Carbon sequestration is a process when harmful carbon dioxide in the atmosphere is stored, in this case in trees, and taken out of the atmosphere limiting the harm it can create. According to NOAA the rainforest is made of 2.8 million square miles of jungle, and it contains around 123 billion tons of carbon. However, because of overexploitation and deforestatio, the rainforest is being burned and cut down. When this happens, all the carbon stored in the trees for thousands of years is released into the atmosphere. This leads to increased temperatures and quickens the impacts of climate change. Specifically accordin to Biology Insights, the biggest cause of deforestation is from cattle ranching. Farming not only causes trees to be cut down, but can also cause soil erosion and chemicals to be runoff into nearby streams, which would impact drinking water and cause illness. If deforestation continues to occur there could be disatrous impacts on the climate. In fact, scientists predict that if all 400 billion trees are cut down there could be around 5 years worth of emissions released at once. The same source also says that of the entire forest, 20% has been lost and another 20% has been impacted in general. Because of all the damage done to the rainforest, there is a large viewable portion of the rainforest that is no longer lush with forestry as it was years ago.
